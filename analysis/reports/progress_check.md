# ③ Progress Check

Generated by `python -m analysis.progress_check`. Predicts the **scholarship outcome**
(evaluation-year total ≥ 60) from **earlier** years only.

## Not circular by construction

`total_score` is a deterministic function of the seven items in the *same* year, so
predicting it from those items would be arithmetic, not modelling. `build_dataset()`
keeps only students whose evaluation year is strictly later than the feature years and
raises otherwise.

## Honest metrics

|      |   n_train |   n_test |   base_rate |   auc_test |   auc_train |   accuracy_test |   brier_test |   log_loss_test |   majority_baseline |
|:-----|----------:|---------:|------------:|-----------:|------------:|----------------:|-------------:|----------------:|--------------------:|
| g1   |     15105 |     5035 |      0.7374 |     0.8664 |      0.8698 |          0.8214 |       0.1238 |          0.3875 |              0.7374 |
| g1g2 |     11755 |     3919 |      0.6827 |     0.872  |      0.8829 |          0.8216 |       0.1288 |          0.4051 |              0.6827 |

**Read these carefully.** `majority_baseline` is what you get by predicting 'passes'
for everyone. AUC is the discrimination that matters for ranking who to help first;
Brier score and the calibration figure say whether the probabilities can be believed as
probabilities. A three-year-ahead projection from one sitting is genuinely uncertain and
the numbers are reported so that uncertainty is visible rather than smoothed over.

## Risk bands

- `at_risk` — pass probability < 0.50
- `watch` — 0.50 to 0.75
- `on_track` — ≥ 0.75

A flag is a prompt to offer support earlier, never a prediction shown to a student as
fact, and never a ranking. Wording that reaches a student stays directional.
